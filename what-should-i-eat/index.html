Why Is It So Hard to Decide What to Eat?

We've all been there: staring into the abyss of a delivery app or an open fridge, completely unable to decide what to eat. The "what do you want for dinner" problem is a classic example of choice paralysis. Having too many options actually makes it harder for our brains to make a decision.

Our food decision maker cuts through the noise by drastically limiting your choices based on your general mood. Instead of choosing between 50 restaurants, you just choose "cheap" or "healthy," and we assign you a specific category. Even if you reject the suggestion, that gut reaction ("Ugh, no, I don't want a salad") helps you narrow down what you actually DO want.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does the food picker work?

Each mood maps to a curated set of food-themed verdicts. The result is deterministic based on the time and mood you select.

Are these real restaurant recommendations?

No — we suggest categories and moods, not specific restaurants. Think of it as deciding WHAT to eat, not WHERE.

Can I use this for meal planning?

It's designed for spontaneous decisions, but honestly, hitting 'Surprise Me' five times for a week of dinners isn't the worst strategy.

Why do I keep getting the same answer?

The algorithm is pseudo-deterministic. Try a different mood for variety, or come back a bit later!
